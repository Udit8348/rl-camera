You are building a Unity MVP called CameraLab.

Your job is to produce a clean, extensible, Unity-based photography training simulator that is actually useful for teaching beginners. The top two priorities are:

1. Extensibility:
- The MVP must be built on a foundation that can later support more scenes, more lesson types, more subjects, more optics realism, and more game systems without major rewrites.
- The codebase must be data-driven where possible and organized around stable core abstractions.

2. Educational accuracy:
- The MVP does NOT need physically perfect optics.
- It DOES need to be correct enough to teach the right intuition.
- Cause-and-effect must be trustworthy:
  - aperture affects brightness and depth of field
  - shutter speed affects brightness and motion blur / camera shake risk
  - ISO affects brightness and noise
  - focal length affects framing and shake sensitivity
  - focus distance affects sharpness plane
  - white balance affects color cast relative to scene lighting

Build the MVP so it teaches these correctly and visibly.

The code should be production-minded, modular, readable, inspector-friendly, and easy to tune.

==================================================
1. PRODUCT DEFINITION
==================================================

CameraLab is a Unity desktop MVP for learning digital camera basics through interactive simulation.

The player enters a curated scene, adjusts camera settings, sees the live preview change, captures a shot, and gets feedback explaining why the image is too dark, blurry, noisy, or color-shifted.

This is NOT:
- a full photography game
- a pro camera brand emulator
- a physically exact ray-traced optics simulator
- an open world game

This IS:
- a focused photography learning sandbox
- a curated set of scenes
- a system that teaches exposure, blur, focus, and white balance
- a strong technical foundation for future expansion

B. EDUCATIONAL ACCURACY REQUIREMENT

The MVP should be “instructionally accurate,” meaning:
- It teaches the right directional effects and tradeoffs.
- It is internally consistent.
- It is tunable.
- It produces plausible photography outcomes.

Where realism is simplified, prefer simple, explicit heuristic models that preserve correct intuition.

Example:
- DOF can be approximated with a heuristic depth blur model.
- Motion blur can be approximated with shutter-speed-driven blur.
- ISO noise can be approximated with a noise shader and shadow weighting.
- White balance can be approximated with temperature mismatch tinting.

But the causal relationships must remain correct.

7. SIMULATION RULES AND HEURISTICS
==================================================

Use formulas that are simple, readable, tunable, and directionally correct.

--------------------------------------------------
7.1 EXPOSURE MODEL
--------------------------------------------------

Use a simplified exposure relationship.

Recommended approach:
- Compute camera exposure value approximately from aperture, shutter, and ISO
- Compare against scene ambient EV
- Convert difference into exposureOffsetStops

Suggested approximation:
cameraEV100 = log2((apertureF * apertureF) / shutterSeconds)
cameraEV = cameraEV100 - log2(iso / 100.0)
exposureOffsetStops = sceneAmbientEV - cameraEV

Interpretation:
- positive offset means underexposed relative to scene
- negative offset means overexposed
- near zero is good exposure

Also derive:
exposureNormalized01 = value based on closeness to ideal exposure

Make sure the signs and comments are clear and consistent throughout the code.

Educational requirement:
- opening aperture should brighten image
- slower shutter should brighten image
- higher ISO should brighten image
- “equivalent exposure” settings should produce similar brightness but different blur/noise/DOF tradeoffs

--------------------------------------------------
7.2 DEPTH OF FIELD MODEL
--------------------------------------------------

We do not need exact optics, but we do need correct intuition.

The DOF / blur model should increase background blur when:
- aperture is wider (smaller f-number)
- focal length is longer
- subject is closer
- background is farther behind subject
- focus is correctly placed on subject

Suggested heuristic:
- compute subjectFocusDelta = abs(focusDistance - subjectDistance)
- compute subjectSharpness from subjectFocusDelta
- compute background separation = max(0, backgroundDistance - subjectDistance)
- compute blur potential from:
  - inverse aperture
  - focal length factor
  - inverse subject distance
  - background separation

Example directional model:
apertureFactor = remap(1 / apertureF)
focalLengthFactor = remap(focalLengthMm)
distanceFactor = remap(1 / subjectDistanceMeters)
backgroundSeparationFactor = remap(backgroundDistanceMeters - subjectDistanceMeters)

dofBlurStrength = combined weighted function of these factors

Important:
- keep focus sharpness and background blur related but not identical
- if focus is wrong, subject should soften even if blur potential is high
- if aperture is narrow, background should remain sharper

--------------------------------------------------
7.3 FOCUS MODEL
--------------------------------------------------

Focus must be taught clearly.

Implement:
focusErrorMeters = abs(focusDistanceMeters - subjectDistanceMeters)

Then compute:
focusSharpness01 = function decreasing with focus error and becoming more forgiving at narrower apertures

Educational requirement:
- focusing closer or farther than subject should reduce subject sharpness
- wider apertures should be less forgiving
- narrower apertures should be more forgiving

--------------------------------------------------
7.4 SUBJECT MOTION BLUR MODEL
--------------------------------------------------

Motion blur should increase when:
- shutter speed gets slower
- subject speed is higher

Suggested heuristic:
motionExposure = shutterSeconds * subjectSpeedMetersPerSecond
subjectMotionBlur01 = remap(motionExposure with tuning curve)

Educational requirement:
- fast shutter freezes movement better
- slow shutter blurs moving subjects more

--------------------------------------------------
7.5 CAMERA SHAKE MODEL
--------------------------------------------------

Camera shake blur should increase when:
- shutter speed gets slower
- focal length gets longer
- shakeDifficulty is higher
- tripod is off

Suggested heuristic:
if tripodEnabled:
    cameraShakeBlur01 = near zero
else:
    shakeRisk = shutterSeconds * focalLengthMm * shakeDifficulty * tuningScalar
    cameraShakeBlur01 = remap(shakeRisk)

Educational requirement:
- longer focal lengths are harder to handhold
- tripod stabilizes image
- slow shutter handheld is risky

--------------------------------------------------
7.6 TOTAL BLUR MODEL
--------------------------------------------------

Combine subject motion blur, camera shake blur, and focus softness into a total blur score.

But preserve separate metrics internally for coaching.

Example:
focusBlur01 = 1 - focusSharpness01
totalBlur01 = weighted combine of:
- focusBlur01
- subjectMotionBlur01
- cameraShakeBlur01

--------------------------------------------------
7.7 NOISE MODEL
--------------------------------------------------

Noise should increase when:
- ISO is higher
- image is strongly underexposed and then effectively “pushed”
- darker scenes tend to reveal noise more

Suggested heuristic:
isoFactor = log2(iso / 100.0)
underexposurePenalty = max(0, exposureOffsetStops)
noiseAmount01 = weighted combine of isoFactor and underexposurePenalty, clamped 0..1

Educational requirement:
- raising ISO increases noise
- underexposing and trying to rescue the shot should look noisier

--------------------------------------------------
7.8 WHITE BALANCE MODEL
--------------------------------------------------

White balance should be modeled as mismatch between scene lighting temperature and chosen preset.

Create WB presets:
- Auto
- Daylight
- Cloudy
- Shade
- Tungsten
- Fluorescent

Map each preset to an approximate temperature value.

Suggested reference:
- Daylight ~ 5500K
- Cloudy ~ 6500K
- Shade ~ 7500K
- Tungsten ~ 3200K
- Fluorescent ~ 4200K
- Auto = sceneColorTemperatureK for MVP or near it

Then:
whiteBalanceMismatch01 = normalized difference between sceneColorTemperatureK and selected preset target

Educational requirement:
- wrong WB should produce visible warm/cool tint mismatch
- correct preset should neutralize scene better

- “The subject is blurred by motion. Use a faster shutter speed.”
- “The subject is out of focus. Move the focus distance closer to the subject.”
- “The background is too sharp. Use a wider aperture or increase subject-background separation.”
- “The image is noisy because ISO is high and the shot is underexposed.”
- “The white balance is too warm for this scene.”

Important:
- Keep the coaching tied to actual underlying metrics
- Avoid vague messages
- Make feedback educational, not just judgmental
